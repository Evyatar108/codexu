# Brainstorm synthesis — auto-notify the crew LEAD when a member crashes

**Task:** `crews-member-crash-auto-notify-lead` (crews plugin, ai-developer-toolkit submodule).

**The gap (confirmed against source):** crash *detection* exists and is high quality (`hooks/health.js::getMemberHealth` → `state:"dead"` when launcher pid is gone, or no pid + heartbeat stale past `HEARTBEAT_STALE_MS = 5min`), but it is **pull-only** — the only callers are `snapshotCrew`/`list-members` (`hooks/crews.js:149`) and `resume-crew`. A dead member can't self-report (a `kind=blocked` report needs a live process), and the lead's armed listener only wakes on **mail delivery**. The delivery seam already exists (`appendSystemMailbox(lead, crew, cwd, env, {kind})` is how `member-joined` / `member-left` / `member-reply` / `proactive-report` wake the lead). The work is the *sweep* that joins detection to delivery, plus a dedupe latch — **and choosing a trigger that actually fires while the lead is idle.**

## ⚠️ The finding that reshapes the seed's proposed mechanism

The seed proposed "piggyback on the lead listener's existing wake cycle (idle-timeout + every delivery)." The Devil's Advocate lens proved (and I verified in `lib/listener-loop.js`) that this is **broken for the exact case that motivated the task**:

- In normal codexu operation the lead listener is armed **indefinitely** — `timeoutMs === null` (`lib/listener-loop.js:127-134`), so the idle-timeout `bail()` is **never installed** (`:428`, `:440-442`). The codexu AGENTS.md "arm indefinitely / never pass `--timeout-ms`" rule guarantees this.
- The listener **delivers-then-exits**: `deliver()` consumes the mailbox and `finish()`es (`:356-393`). So "every delivery" is a once-per-lifecycle event, and it only happens when *some other* mail arrives — a hard crash writes **no** mail.
- Therefore a sweep tied to `initial` / `watch` / `poll-delivery` / `timeout` **cannot notice a quiet-period crash**. The member dies while the lead is asleep waiting for mail, and detection latency is **unbounded** — which is precisely the 2026-06-06 (two brainstorm members died, lead never noticed) and the ~9h-dark incidents.
- The **only** periodic hook inside an indefinite listener is the heartbeat `setInterval` (~10s, `:305-353`), which today only refreshes the lead's own manifest. A sweep that fires during a quiet idle lead **must** live on a periodic timer — i.e. an explicit (throttled) background monitor. Calling that "not polling" is semantic hair-splitting; the honest framing is *daemon-internal monitoring, not lead-MODEL polling* (which is what the "always listeners, never poll" rule actually targets).

A second structural fact: the lead's listener subprocess is **single-actor today** — it parses one `name`/`crew`/`cwd` and owns exactly one mailbox + one listener epoch. It *can* enumerate siblings (`listNames` in `hooks/actors.js:1144-1153`, `getMemberHealth` per member), but that adds cross-actor responsibility + O(members) synchronous filesystem/`process.kill` checks to a component deliberately kept small — on a Windows box that has already shipped many releases hardening lock/write fragility under Defender pressure.

## Candidate directions (mechanism — the primary architectural choice)

### D-001: Heartbeat-tick liveness monitor in the lead listener (RECOMMENDED core)
- Contributing lenses: [copilot, devils-advocate]
- Why this might work: It is the **only** mechanism that wakes a *quiet idle* lead — the case that motivated the task. Piggyback the sweep on the existing heartbeat `setInterval` in the lead's armed listener, **throttled** to a slow cadence (e.g. ≤ once / 60s, not every 10s). On a high-confidence `alive|quiet|unknown → dead` transition for a member *incarnation*, append a `member-crashed` system envelope to the lead's own mailbox **before** the listener's own `consumeMailbox`/exit so the crash row rides out in the same wake (or self-deliver), waking the lead immediately. Reuses the trusted mailbox/review channel end-to-end.
- Risks / friction: It is an explicit background monitor (reframes — not eliminates — the "no poll" rule; defend it as daemon-internal, not model polling). Adds O(members) manifest + `launcher.pid` + `process.kill` + mailbox-peek reads per throttled tick → more Windows FS/lock pressure; must respect manifest/mailbox lock ordering to avoid self-deadlock when the lead writes its own mailbox. The listener gains cross-actor roster responsibility it doesn't have today. Ordering is load-bearing: sweep-after-consume reintroduces the delayed-delivery shape v3.12.0 just made observable.
- Cheapest validation: Prototype a throttled sweep on heartbeat tick that detects ONE synthetic dead-member transition and writes a single `member-crashed` envelope; assert the armed lead listener wakes **exactly once**, and that a `quiet`/`unknown` member produces **zero** envelopes.
- Disconfirming observation: In a crash drill the lead is not woken through the mailbox during a quiet period, OR gets duplicate notifications across repeated ticks, OR the O(members) per-tick IO measurably regresses listener stability under Defender pressure.

### D-002: Dying-gasp self-report + sweep backstop
- Contributing lenses: [devils-advocate]
- Why this might work: Convert "no signal from a corpse" into a "dying gasp." The member's own listener/launcher best-effort emits a `member-crashed` envelope to the lead on **unexpected** exit — a Node `process.on('exit'|'uncaughtException')` in listener-side code, and the PowerShell launcher (which already owns the engine lifecycle for hard-terminate/tab-close) on a non-clean engine exit — giving **near-zero detection latency** for the graceful-ish crash classes. A slower high-confidence sweep (D-001 or D-003) is then only the backstop for hard kills.
- Risks / friction: Handlers do **not** fire on `taskkill /T`, OS crash, power loss, or the exact synchronous lock-failure class (UNKNOWN/-4094) that killed the two 2026-06-06 members before any JS/PowerShell cleanup could run — i.e. it can miss the very incident that motivated this. Engine/launcher-specific; must suppress false notices on intentional `/stop-member`, `/resume-crew`, and clean completion (consult manifest shutdown fields + exit code).
- Cheapest validation: Add an `uncaughtException`/`exit` hook to the member listener that appends a `member-crashed` envelope; verify it fires on a thrown-error exit and is **suppressed** on a clean `/stop-member`. Separately confirm it does NOT fire on `taskkill /T` (documents the backstop gap).
- Disconfirming observation: The dominant real-world crash classes on this box (hard kill / lock-bug / OS-level) skip the handler, so the gasp covers few actual incidents and a sweep backstop is still mandatory.

### D-003: Lead-model turn-boundary hook sweep
- Contributing lenses: [devils-advocate]
- Why this might work: Run the health-transition sweep in the **lead's** PreToolUse / Stop / SessionStart hooks (or the lead command path), where roster + state-cwd context is already natural and the listener-loop hot path is untouched. It is also the natural home for **latch-reset hygiene** and pairs cleanly with the existing review-mail flow.
- Risks / friction: It **cannot wake an idle lead** after a quiet-period crash — detection waits until the lead has another turn/tool boundary (operator prompt or unrelated mail). So it does **not** by itself satisfy the push-notification goal; it is a safer *backstop* + latch-reset layer, not a standalone solution.
- Cheapest validation: Add a transition check at lead Stop that injects a `member-crashed` context line; confirm it surfaces on the lead's next turn for a dead member and is idempotent across turns via the latch.
- Disconfirming observation: An idle lead (armed listener, no turns) never runs the hook, so the worst-case latency equals "time until the operator next prompts the lead" — re-confirming D-003 alone is insufficient.

> **Layered reading:** D-001 covers the *idle-armed* lead (the gap); D-003 covers the *actively-working* lead at turn boundaries; D-002 gives low-latency for graceful crashes. A robust shipped feature is plausibly **D-001 as the load-bearing core + D-003 for latch-reset/turn-boundary backstop**, with D-002 as an optional fast-path. The operator should pick the v1 scope.

## Cross-cutting scope decisions (apply to whichever mechanism is chosen)

Both lenses converge on these — recommended defaults shown, operator confirms/adjusts:

1. **Hard-crash vs stalled/idle — THE key scope decision (Q6).** **Recommend: hard-crash high-confidence-`dead` is the v1 CORE; DEFER stalled-detection as a separate feature.** The ~9h-dark case (`pidAlive && heartbeat-fresh && lastTurnAt frozen`) is classified `alive`/`quiet`, not `dead`, and is a **false-positive minefield**: a member legitimately waiting on its armed listener for the next message looks *identical* to a stall (fresh listener heartbeat, frozen `lastTurnAt`, zero queued mail, no active tool = **healthy idle**, not a stall); long legit builds/tool-calls freeze `lastTurnAt` for hours; the health module is engine-agnostic and can't see active-tool state. **Honest framing:** hard-crash-only is a *partial* fix — it does NOT solve the 9h-dark class (the system reports false signs of life). The right stalled primitive is a *different* event (`member-needs-attention` / `member-stalled`), with a longer threshold, an explicit healthy-idle carve-out (e.g. `listenerState==='armed' && queuedMailboxCount===0 && lastKind!=='progress'`), and a *weaker* default action (a gentle auto-nudge — append a poke envelope to wake the member's own listener — rather than a crash page). Keep it out of v1; file it as a follow-up.
2. **Operator visibility (Q5).** **Recommend: lead-only in the core; operator escalation is a phase-2 config knob, NOT bundled.** A member crash is first a *lead* coordination event; auto-paging the operator (`escalate-to-operator` / `operator-escalations.jsonl` / `appendOperatorMailbox`) for every hard-dead member bypasses the lead's triage role and risks alert fatigue + ownership ambiguity. If wanted, gate it: operator escalation only if no lead listener is currently armed, or the crash stays unreviewed for N minutes, or the member was spawned operator-visible.
3. **Envelope kind.** **Recommend: a NEW `member-crashed` ENVELOPE kind, analogous to `member-left`** (NOT a turn-tag report kind — the two enums are distinct: `kindEnum` in `hooks/protocol/envelope.js:63-88` vs `REPORT_KINDS`/`VALID_KINDS`). Three explicit decisions required: add to `kindEnum`; add to `DEFAULT_REVIEW_KINDS` (`hooks/protocol/review-required.js:1-12`) **only if** the lead must review-mail before continuing; and add to the strict-ack auto-exempt set (`hooks/stop.js` `ACK_EXEMPT_KINDS`, ~`:189-196`) if it is notification-only — otherwise you trade "silent crash" for "lead blocked by an ack chore for a system notice." Likely review-required **and** ack-exempt (like `member-left`). It will also need a `PAYLOAD_RULES` entry if it carries required fields.
4. **Dedupe latch — generation-scoped, not boolean.** **Recommend a generation/incarnation key** `{ memberName, sessionId, launcherPid, launcherStartedAt, deadReason }`, notify only when the current dead signature differs from the last-notified signature. A bare `crashNotified: true` either suppresses a valid re-crash after `/resume-crew` or needs brittle manual clearing. **Latch home is an open question** (see below): the dead member's manifest (written by the sweeper under the member manifest lock — but races `/resume-crew`'s `repairManifestForResume` which also takes that lock and rewrites `listenerState:'recoverable'`/`lastListenerPid:null`), vs a lead/crew-level `liveness-notifications.json`/incidents file (cleaner for multi-lead crews, but a new state file + lock discipline). Reset must be wired into `repairManifestForResume` and clear-member cleanup regardless of home. Note: a new top-level manifest field churns the exact-count test (`tests/protocol-manifest.test.js`, currently pins `manifestFields.length === 51`); a `manifest.custom.*` bag avoids field-count churn but is still protocol.

## False-negative honesty (detection trust)
The conservative posture (notify only on existing `dead`; never on `quiet`/`unknown`) is correct, but be explicit about what it **misses**: `EPERM` on `process.kill(pid,0)` is treated as alive (`hooks/health.js:18-21`); `isProcessAlive` checks only pid existence, not image/parentage/start-time, so a **recycled PID** can mask a real death (stop-member has recycle guards; health does not); a wedged launcher with a live pid + stale heartbeat is `quiet`, not `dead`. Product copy should say "notify on **high-confidence hard-dead transitions**," not "notify when a member crashes."

## Recommendation
**D-001 (heartbeat-tick liveness monitor in the lead listener)** is the recommended **core mechanism** — it is the only candidate that wakes a *quiet idle* lead, which is the failure mode the task was filed for. Pair it with **D-003** for turn-boundary backstop + latch-reset hygiene; treat **D-002 (dying-gasp)** as an optional fast-path. Across all mechanisms, ship **hard-crash high-confidence-`dead` only** in v1 and **defer stalled-detection** as a separate `member-needs-attention` feature; keep **operator escalation** as a phase-2 knob; use a **new `member-crashed` envelope kind** + a **generation-scoped latch**.

## Open questions to carry into planning
1. Is the product requirement *immediate* quiet-period crash notification (→ requires the D-001 periodic monitor / accept a background timer), or is "surface crash state at the lead's next wake" acceptable (→ D-003 suffices, simpler)?
2. Is the "always listeners, never poll" rule absolute even for **daemon internals**, or is a throttled in-listener monitor an acceptable carve-out (it's daemon monitoring, not lead-model polling)?
3. Latch home + exact generation key, and the precise reset wiring in `resume-crew` repair + clear-member cleanup — member manifest vs lead/crew-level incidents file?
4. `member-crashed`: review-required AND strict-ack-exempt (like `member-left`), or force the lead to ack?
5. Defer the 9h-dark stalled case to a separate `member-needs-attention`/auto-nudge feature (recommended), or in-scope behind a default-off threshold?
6. Acceptance criterion for v1: exactly one review-required lead notification per high-confidence alive→dead incarnation transition, zero notifications for `quiet`/`unknown`?
